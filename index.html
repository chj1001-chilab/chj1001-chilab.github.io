Research Interests

Over five years at Asan Medical Center, I built a series of clinical prediction models, for warfarin dosing, diuretic response, and cardiovascular outcomes. Along the way, I noticed that my work kept centering on model performance, while the harder question was whether a model answers a problem clinicians actually need solved. I am pursuing a PhD to learn how to find research questions with real clinical value, and to build the methods that solve them.

02

Research Experience

Three first-author studies on clinical prediction from electronic health records.

2023 · Scientific Reports · First author

Machine learning for warfarin discharge dosing

What I did

Predicted the warfarin discharge dose from 17 routine EMR features available within two days of admission (n = 3,168), without genetic data.

→
Result

Internal validation MAE 0.9 mg vs 1.3 mg for physicians' initial dose; in a reader study, five physicians' doses on 40 cases agreed poorly with one another (ICC 0.36).

→
What I learned

On external MIMIC-III data (n = 891), the advantage over physicians did not hold. A model that works at one hospital still has to earn its place at the next.

Paper ↗

2024 · Scientific Reports · First author

Forecasting weight change for diuretic dose adjustment

What I did

Built TSFD-LSTM, a bidirectional LSTM with attention, to forecast 48-hour weight change in heart failure patients receiving diuretics (4,720 patients).

→
Result

85.3% of forecasts within 1 kg (MAE 0.56 kg), compared with 52.7–61.2% for Transformer, neural-network, and random-forest baselines.

→
What I learned

Forecasting how a patient responds to a treatment sits closer to a clinical decision than predicting the patient's current state.

Paper ↗

2026 · Under review · First author

Locally fine-tuned large language models vs. machine learning for tabular clinical prediction

What I did

Fine-tuned four open-weight LLMs with LoRA inside the hospital on health records serialized as text, against machine-learning baselines on three tasks: warfarin dosing, one-year major adverse cardiac events, and five-year post-transplant mortality.

→
Result

Discrimination was close to the gradient-boosted baselines, with numerically higher point estimates for most models, but calibration was generally poorer for mortality, and warfarin error was numerically higher than XGBoost.

→
What I learned

Similar discrimination did not necessarily mean reliable or deployable clinical value.
